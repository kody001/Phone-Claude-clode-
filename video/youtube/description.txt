🔬 THE REAL SCIENCE IN THIS VIDEO
The story is fiction, but the elevator safety facts and body stuff in it are real. Here's where they come from:

• Elevators hang from several steel cables, and just one can hold the car. If it ever moves too fast, emergency brakes grab the guide rails.
How Elevators Work (HowStuffWorks): https://science.howstuffworks.com/transport/engines-equipment/elevator.htm
High-Rise Safety Systems (Otis): https://www.otis.com/tools-resources/high-rise-safety-systems

• Elevators aren't airtight, so you won't run out of air if you're stuck.
5 Steps to Take If Your Elevator Stops (Otis): https://www.otis.com/en/us/w/5-steps-to-take-if-your-elevator-stops
Elevator Safety (UC Irvine Facilities Management): https://www.fm.uci.edu/work-units/elevator/index.php

• Press the alarm or call button, stay inside, and never try to climb out through the ceiling. The inside of a stopped elevator is the safest place to be.
The Dos and Don'ts of Elevator Safety (Safety+Health Magazine, quoting the Elevator Escalator Safety Foundation): https://www.safetyandhealthmagazine.com/articles/16645-the-dos-and-donts-of-elevator-safety
Elevator Failure (Northeastern University Police): https://nupd.northeastern.edu/safety/general-safety-tips/emergency-guidebook/elevator-failure/

• Fear of small spaces (claustrophobia) is common, and lifts are a classic trigger.
Claustrophobia (NHS): https://www.nhs.uk/mental-health/conditions/claustrophobia/

• Slow, counted breathing, like box breathing, can help calm panic.
Box Breathing (Cleveland Clinic): https://health.clevelandclinic.org/box-breathing-benefits

• Stomach growling is normal digestive noise, and it's loudest when your stomach is empty.
Stomach Growling and Other Digestive Noise (Harvard Health): https://www.health.harvard.edu/digestive-health/stomach-growling-and-other-digestive-noise

If you're ever really stuck, follow the instructions posted in the elevator and your building's emergency contacts.
